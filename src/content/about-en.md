# Who is Neuroweave? What do we do?

## What is Neuroweave?

Neuroweave is a Chinese-language knowledge-sharing group focused on neurodiversity and neurodivergence. We hope to make resources, experiences, and discussions that are often difficult to access in Chinese-language contexts more available to a wider audience.

Through translation, writing, interviews, and community exchange, we connect people from different backgrounds through an open and decentralized approach to collaboration, and explore the different possibilities of neurodiversity together.

We also hope to create more possibilities for people to understand themselves and others, and encourage everyone to choose the language and narratives that best fit their own experiences and needs.

## What does Neuroweave do?

### Translating information, organizing resources, and sharing experiences

- We follow research, writing, and personal narratives related to neurodiversity and neurodivergence, and try to turn this information into language that is easier to understand and discuss.

- We also organize, introduce, and discuss relevant tools and toolkits that may help people understand their own experiences and navigate everyday life.

- Readers are welcome to ask questions or leave comments. We will use the topics that people are interested in to help guide our future reading, resource organization, and introductions to relevant materials.

### Documenting experiences and exploring language

- We are interested in how different people experience their bodies, process information, understand their own experiences, and interact with their environments. We hope that different ways of living, as well as different choices about how to live, can leave traces in the record of human experience.

- As we organize resources and introduce different experiences, we try to provide language and frameworks that can help people describe and understand their own experiences. However, we do not believe that there is one single correct way to express an experience.

If certain ways of describing an experience do not fit yours, you are welcome to offer a different description. You can leave a comment, send us a private message, or simply keep your own judgment:

> "I don't like this way of describing it. I think my experience is closer to another description."

We hope that language can help people understand their experiences, rather than limit them.

### Exchange and connection

- We document conversations with other self-advocacy groups, nonprofit organizations, and practitioners, hoping to learn about experiences, research, and practices that have developed in different regions and communities.

- We also hope to provide a relatively quiet space for exchange, with less social pressure, where participants can choose how they participate according to their own needs.
